1. Tillamook Bay (Striped Bass, Bluefish, Fluke, Weakfish, Winter Flounder, Tautog)

Tillamook Bay, Oregon

Tillamook Bay offers excellent fishing in the Coast & Coastal Lakes of Oregon with good public access.

  • ⚠ This water: Open all year for hatchery steelhead.
  • ⚠ This water: Open for hatchery spring Chinook salmon inside the jetty tips Jan 1 - Jul 31.
  • ⚠ This water: Open for fall Chinook salmon Aug 1 - Dec 31.
  • ⚠ This water: Closed to sturgeon angling upstream from the Hwy 131 bridge.

Boat Launch: Garibaldi Harbor →

3. Coos Bay (Striped Bass, Bluefish, Fluke, Weakfish, Winter Flounder, Tautog)

Coos Bay, Oregon

Coos Bay offers excellent fishing in the Coast & Coastal Lakes of Oregon with good public access.

  • ⚠ This water: Open all year for hatchery steelhead.
  • ⚠ This water: Open all year for Chinook salmon.
  • ⚠ This water: Isthmus Slough above MP 3 on Hwy 42 is open to youth angling (ages 17 and under) and Oregon Disabilities Hunting and Fishing Permit holders only; Anti-Snagging Gear Restriction: Leader length must be between 18 and 36 inches (See [Anti-Snagging Gear Restriction Rules](https://www.eregulations.com/oregon/fishing/general-statewide-regulations)).

Fishing Tips for the Coast & Coastal Lakes

  • Get a licence — All anglers need a valid state fishing licence — check your state's wildlife agency site.
  • Check the weather & water conditions — always check forecasts and wind before heading out, especially on big water.
  • Know the regulations — size and catch limits vary by water body in Oregon; check the local regulations first.
  • Best times — early morning and late evening are prime. Many Coast & Coastal Lakes waters fish best in spring and fall.
  • Use the right gear — match your rod, line, and tackle to the species you're chasing and the water you're fishing.
