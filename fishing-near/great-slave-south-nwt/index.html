Fishing in Great Slave Lake & Southern NWT

Explore fishing in the Great Slave Lake & Southern NWT of Northwest Territories. Great Slave Lake, Slave River, Hay Rive

Great Slave Lake is the deepest lake in North America. The west basin near Hay River offers excellent lake trout, northern pike, and Arctic grayling fishing. Lake trout in the 10-20 pound range are common. Shallow bays hold good pike populations.

1. Great Slave Lake (Arctic Grayling, Lake Trout, Northern Pike)

Hay River / Fort Providence area

The North Arm of Great Slave Lake offers lake trout, northern pike, and Arctic grayling fishing delta.

2. Slave River (Northern Pike, Walleye)

Fort Smith area

The Slave River offers excellent walleye and northern pike fishing flowing from Lake Athabasca into Great Slave Lake. Walleye in the 2-5 pound range are common, with larger fish possible. The river is also famous for world-class whitewater kayaking.

Fishing Tips for the Great Slave Lake & Southern NWT

  • Get a licence — All anglers need a valid fishing licence for your province — buy online before heading out.
  • Check the weather & water conditions — always check forecasts and wind before heading out, especially on big water.
  • Know the regulations — size and catch limits vary by water body in Northwest Territories; check the local regulations first.
  • Best times — early morning and late evening are prime. Many Great Slave Lake & Southern NWT waters fish best in spring and fall.
  • Use the right gear — match your rod, line, and tackle to the species you're chasing and the water you're fishing.
