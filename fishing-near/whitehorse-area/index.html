Fishing in the Whitehorse Area

Explore fishing in the Whitehorse Area of Yukon. Yukon River, Fish Lake, Lake Laberge, Marsh Lake, Tagish Lake, Schwatka

The Yukon River through Whitehorse is famous for producing the world's largest Chinook salmon, with fish exceeding 60 pounds. Fishing is primarily from boats out of Whitehorse. Arctic grayling and chum salmon are also abundant. The river offers a unique urban wilderness fishing experience.

1. Yukon River (Arctic Grayling, Chinook Salmon, Chum Salmon)

Whitehorse area

The Yukon River through Whitehorse is famous for producing the world's largest Chinook salmon, with fish exceeding 60 pounds. Fishing is primarily from boats out of Whitehorse. Arctic grayling and chum salmon are also abundant. The river offers a unique urban wilderness fishing experience.

  • Arctic grayling · Limit: daily 5 / possession 10 · Only one may be longer than 40 cm

2. Fish Lake (Arctic Grayling, Lake Trout)

Fish Lake Road, Yukon

Fish Lake is one of the most popular fishing spots offering excellent lake trout and Arctic grayling fishing. The lake is easily accessible via the Fish Lake Road and provides good shore and boat fishing opportunities.

3. Lake Laberge (Lake Trout, Northern Pike)

Lake Laberge area, Yukon

Lake Laberge offers excellent lake trout and northern pike fishing in a large lake setting. Famous from Robert Service's poem "The Cremation of Sam McGee," the lake is accessible from the North Klondike Highway and provides good boat fishing.

Fishing Tips for the Whitehorse Area

  • Get a licence — All anglers need a valid fishing licence for your province — buy online before heading out.
  • Check the weather & water conditions — always check forecasts and wind before heading out, especially on big water.
  • Know the regulations — size and catch limits vary by water body in Yukon; check the local regulations first.
  • Best times — early morning and late evening are prime. Many Whitehorse Area waters fish best in spring and fall.
  • Use the right gear — match your rod, line, and tackle to the species you're chasing and the water you're fishing.
