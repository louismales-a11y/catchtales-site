Fishing in the Catskills & Hudson Valley

Explore fishing in the Catskills & Hudson Valley of New York. Beaverkill River, Esopus Creek, Hudson River, Neversink Ri

The Beaverkill River is the birthplace of American fly fishing, offering world-class wild brown and rainbow trout fishing in the Catskill Mountains.

1. Beaverkill River (Brown Trout, Rainbow Trout, Brook Trout, Smallmouth Bass)

Beaverkill River, Sullivan County

The Beaverkill River is the birthplace of American fly fishing, offering world-class wild brown and rainbow trout fishing in the Catskill Mountains.

  • Largemouth & smallmouth bass · Limit: 5 combined · Min 12 in (some waters different)

Boat Launch: Beaverkill River →

Boat Launch: East Launch →

3. Hudson River (Striped Bass, Smallmouth Bass, Largemouth Bass, Channel Catfish, White Perch, Walleye)

Hudson River, Multiple counties

The Hudson River offers world-class striped bass fishing from New York City to Albany with excellent smallmouth bass in its freshwater reaches.

  • Striped bass (freshwater) · Limit: 1 per day · 18-28 in slot (varies by water)
  • Largemouth & smallmouth bass · Limit: 5 combined · Min 12 in (some waters different)
  • Walleye · Limit: 5 · Min 15 in (some waters 18 in)

Boat Launch: Henry Hudson Park Boat Launch →

4. Neversink River (Brown Trout, Rainbow Trout, Brook Trout, Smallmouth Bass)

Neversink River, Sullivan County

The Neversink River is a world-renowned Catskill trout stream originating from the Neversink Reservoir with wild brown and brook trout.

  • ⚠ This water: From the Guymard Tpke crossing upstream to the Sullivan/Orange County Line _(Stocked-Extended)_ | * April 1-October 15, Daily Limit-3 with no more than 1 longer than 12" * October 16-March 31, Catch and Release only, Artificial lures only
  • Largemouth & smallmouth bass · Limit: 5 combined · Min 12 in (some waters different)

Boat Launch: East Launch →

Fishing Tips for the Catskills & Hudson Valley

  • Get a licence — All anglers need a valid state fishing licence — check your state's wildlife agency site.
  • Check the weather & water conditions — always check forecasts and wind before heading out, especially on big water.
  • Know the regulations — size and catch limits vary by water body in New York; check the local regulations first.
  • Best times — early morning and late evening are prime. Many Catskills & Hudson Valley waters fish best in spring and fall.
  • Use the right gear — match your rod, line, and tackle to the species you're chasing and the water you're fishing.
