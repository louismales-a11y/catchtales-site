Fishing in Manitoba's Central Plains: Portage la Prairie & Delta Marsh

Explore fishing in the Manitoba's Central Plains: Portage la Prairie & Delta Marsh of Manitoba. Portage Reservoir, Delta

The Central Plains region stretches from Portage la Prairie south to the US border and west to the Pembina Valley. The region is defined by the Assiniboine River, the Portage Reservoir, and the vast Delta Marsh on Lake Manitoba's south shore. This is prairie fishing at its best — accessible, productive, and full of variety.

1. Portage Reservoir (Walleye, Northern Pike)

The Portage Reservoir, formed by a dam on the Assiniboine River la Prairie, offers excellent fishing for walleye and northern pike. The reservoir's shallow, productive waters hold healthy fish populations. Walleye in the 2-4 pound range are common. The reservoir has a boat launch and several shore fishing access points. Ice fishing is popular in winter, with walleye remaining active under the ice. The Portage Reservoir is easily accessible from the Trans-Canada Highway.

  • Walleye (Pickerel) & Sauger (either or combined) · Limit: 4 · None may exceed 55 cm
  • Northern Pike (Jackfish) · Limit: 4 · None may exceed 75 cm

Boat Launch: Crescent Lake Kayak Dock →

2. Delta Marsh (Northern Pike, Walleye, Perch)

Delta Marsh, on the south shore of Lake Manitoba, is one of the largest freshwater marshes in Canada. The marsh offers excellent fishing for northern pike, walleye, and yellow perch. The shallow, weedy water provides ideal pike habitat, with fish in the 3-8 pound range common. The marsh is also a world-renowned birdwatching destination, hosting thousands of migrating waterfowl. The Delta Beach area offers access to the marsh and Lake Manitoba. The Delta Marsh is best fished by canoe, kayak, or small boat with a shallow draft.

  • Northern Pike (Jackfish) · Limit: 4 · None may exceed 75 cm
  • Walleye (Pickerel) & Sauger (either or combined) · Limit: 4 · None may exceed 55 cm
  • Yellow Perch · Limit: 25

Boat Launch: Crescent Lake Kayak Dock →

3. Assiniboine River (Portage Area)

The Assiniboine River through the Portage la Prairie area offers good fishing for walleye, channel catfish, and goldeye. The river's current, deep pools, and structure hold fish throughout the summer. Spring and fall are best for walleye. Summer evenings produce excellent catfish fishing. The river is accessible from several parks and bridges in Portage la Prairie.

Boat Launch: Crescent Lake Kayak Dock →

4. Lake Minnewasta (Rainbow Trout, Walleye, Northern Pike)

Lake Minnewasta, Manitoba

Lake Minnewasta is a man-made reservoir in the Pembina Valley. The lake is stocked with rainbow trout that average 1-3 pounds, with larger fish available. The lake also holds walleye and northern pike. Lake Minnewasta has a popular campground, beach, and boat launch, making it a favourite family destination. The lake's clear water and scenic valley setting make it one of the most popular fishing lakes in southern Manitoba. Fishing is best in spring and fall when trout are most active surface.

  • Walleye (Pickerel) & Sauger (either or combined) · Limit: 4 · None may exceed 55 cm
  • Northern Pike (Jackfish) · Limit: 4 · None may exceed 75 cm

Boat Launch: Lake Minnewasta →

5. Pembina Valley Provincial Park (Brook Trout, Rainbow Trout)

Pembina Valley Provincial Park offers trout fishing in a unique valley setting. The Pembina River and its tributaries hold brook trout and rainbow trout. The park's deep river valley provides scenic hiking and camping opportunities. The trout streams are small and best fished with light tackle or fly gear. Spring and early summer offer the best conditions before water levels drop.

Boat Launch: Mount Carmel Dam Boating Access →

Stephenfield Lake offers good fishing for walleye and northern pike. The lake is a man-made reservoir that has developed a healthy fish population. Stephenfield Lake has a campground and boat launch, making it a popular family destination. The lake is smaller than the Portage Reservoir, offering a more intimate fishing experience.

Mary Jane Reservoir, Manitoba

The Spruce Woods area is known for its unique sand dune landscape and the Spirit Sands hiking trails. The reservoir is a good option for anglers exploring the Spruce Woods region and provides a different setting from the prairie lakes typical of the Central Plains.

9. La Salle River (Walleye, Channel Catfish, Goldeye)

The La Salle River flows through the communities of La Salle and Sanford into the Red River. The river offers good fishing for walleye, channel catfish, and goldeye. The river is smaller than the Assiniboine and is well-suited to canoe or kayak fishing. The La Salle River is a hidden gem for anglers based in the Winnipeg area looking for a quick fishing trip.

10. Rat River (Southern)

The Rat River flows through the communities of St. Pierre-Jolys and into the Red River. The upper river holds brook trout, while the lower sections hold walleye and channel catfish. The Rat River is a small, scenic stream that's well-suited to wading and fly fishing. The river is less pressured than waters closer to Winnipeg.

Fishing Tips for the Manitoba's Central Plains: Portage la Prairie & Delta Marsh

  • Get a licence — All anglers need a valid fishing licence for your province — buy online before heading out.
  • Check the weather & water conditions — always check forecasts and wind before heading out, especially on big water.
  • Know the regulations — size and catch limits vary by water body in Manitoba; check the local regulations first.
  • Best times — early morning and late evening are prime. Many Manitoba's Central Plains: Portage la Prairie & Delta Marsh waters fish best in spring and fall.
  • Use the right gear — match your rod, line, and tackle to the species you're chasing and the water you're fishing.
