Georgia has over 4,500 lakes and reservoirs — and zero large natural lakes. Every major lake in Georgia is a man-made reservoir, from massive Lake Lanier to the mountain lakes of the Blue Ridge. The state also offers Atlantic coast fishing and world-class trout waters in the north.

Fishing Regulations

  • It is unlawful to possess more than 50 total game fish (except channel/flathead catfish); once the daily limit of a species is taken, fishing for it must stop.
  • No closed season for freshwater fishing except: Flint/Chattahoochee/Spring Creeks closed to striped bass and spear fishing May 1 - Oct 31; Lake Seminole fish refuges closed May 1 - Oct 31.
  • Noodling/grabbling season: March 1 - July 15 statewide.
  • Sturgeon may not be taken in fresh or saltwater in Georgia.
  • Game fish may only be taken by pole and line (except shad, channel/blue/flathead catfish by other legal methods); snagging is illegal.
  • Trout in designated trout waters: 1 pole only. Public Fishing Areas: 2 poles, bass min 14 in unless posted.
  • Live blueback herring bait: legal only in listed lakes (Allatoona, Lanier, West Point, etc.) - illegal elsewhere.
  • Sport trotlines: less than 51 hooks, marked, submerged 3+ ft, no trotlines within 1/2 mile below locks/dams, not allowed on Lake Tobesofkee/State Park lakes/PFAs.
